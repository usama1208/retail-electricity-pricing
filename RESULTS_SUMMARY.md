# PG&E residential pricing reform

Reduce the standard marginal price from $0.22 to **$0.18/kWh** and retain CARE’s one-third discount, yielding **$0.12/kWh**. Set CARE’s fixed charge at **$6/month**. Non-CARE monthly fees are **$51.78**, **$79.66**, and **$111.53** for tract median incomes below $75,000, $75,000–below $125,000, and at least $125,000. These bands are policy assumptions. The reduction closes 28.6% of the gap to the $0.08/kWh social marginal cost benchmark.

The 2019 PG&E price-cut comparison with SCE estimates elasticity at **-0.243** (95% county-clustered normal interval **-0.345 to -0.141**). Household and year-month effects and weather controls cover 2015–2020. Elasticity is the DiD coefficient divided by log(0.22/0.25). CARE is not used for causal identification. The pre-trend test has p = 0.193. **The fixed-charge placebo fails**: its estimate is 0.0383 log points (95% interval 0.0222–0.0543). A border CARE-enrollment imbalance further limits validation. Projections are conditional scenarios, not validated causal forecasts.

For **10,971 PG&E accounts and 128,925 connected months observed in 2023**, the proposal:

- Preserves sample revenue at **$22,698,856.00**, using full-precision fees and financing CARE support within PG&E.
- Raises consumption by **3,711,057 kWh (5.00%)**.
- Raises net consumer surplus by **$705,975.41**, or **$5.48 per connected month**.
- Adds **723.8 metric tonnes CO2e** and **$137,526.19** of climate damage under the historical EPA benchmark (2020 dollars).

Average monthly bills change by **−$57.87 for CARE** and **$12.76 for non-CARE** accounts. Low-use households can lose within each income band; tract income is an imperfect affordability proxy.

**Equal revenue is not equal profit.** At the illustrative private-cost residual of $0.04294/kWh, sample profit falls $159,358.36. A separate profit-neutral calibration requires non-CARE fees of $52.68, $81.05, and $113.47, collecting $159,358.36 more revenue. Actual profit neutrality requires private cost data; cent-rounded fees require reconciliation.

The design improves consumption efficiency and CARE affordability while increasing near-term grid emissions. EV and heat-pump benefits are not estimated. Synthetic data, constant elasticity, unchanged connections, and an unknown population expansion factor limit interpretation. All totals refer to the sample. [Full report and sources](index.html).
